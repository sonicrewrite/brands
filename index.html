How it is made

Five steps, and only the first one costs you time.

Every line in a finished album is language that was actually spoken or written by a real person inside or around the company. Nothing is invented, nothing is embellished, and no copywriter supplies a word that nobody said. That is the whole method, and it is why the result does not sound like advertising. People recognise their own company in it, because it is literally made of them.

01

People talk, and you send us what you have already written

Founders, staff, clients, whoever the album is meant to be made of, recorded for about an hour each, using Clean Language, which means the questions borrow the speaker's own words rather than supplying new ones. Alongside the interviews we read everything the company has already written about itself: decks, internal notes, the careers page, old campaigns, the founder's blog, the values document nobody opens. The written material is often where the phrases a company has been repeating for years without noticing are hiding. Sending it costs you nothing but an afternoon of somebody forwarding files.

02

The language is mapped

Repeated metaphors, the images a company reaches for without noticing, the phrase four different people used unprompted. This is where an album stops being a summary and becomes a portrait.

03

Lyrics are built using only what was said

A copywriter has the whole dictionary. Here there is only what came out of your people's mouths. That constraint is the discipline, and it is the reason the result sounds like you.

04

The music is made in the styles that fit the audience

Chosen for the room it will play in and the people it is for, not for the taste of whoever signed the order.

05

It arrives, and it is yours

Master files, cover, lyric book, and the rights. Two to four weeks depending on how many people were interviewed. Streaming release is optional and entirely your decision.
